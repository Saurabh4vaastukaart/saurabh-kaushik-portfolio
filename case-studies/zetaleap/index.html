Case study 03 · EdTech

Zetaleap

A structured learning product designed to help students and early-career engineers build practical AI skills and become more job-ready.

Product
B2B/B2C SaaS
Domain
AI upskilling
Role
Product Manager
Scope
0-to-1 and iteration
49%

Primary outcome

Weekly active retention increased from 34% to 49%.

Onboarding conversion also increased from 22% to 31%, showing improvement at both the first-value and repeat-use stages of the journey.

In this story The problem Discovery My role Key decisions Measurement What I learned

01 · The problem

Interest in AI was high, but the learning path was unclear.

While hiring engineers, we found that many fresh graduates understood concepts but were not ready to apply AI skills in a real role. Students also struggled to decide where to begin and how different topics connected.

Zetaleap was created to offer a clearer, structured route from learning a concept to practising it and seeing progress.

02 · Discovery

Retention problems became clearer through conversations.

We combined funnel analysis with interviews involving users who had left after one or two months. These conversations helped separate content gaps from journey and motivation problems.

The pattern was not simply that users wanted more material. They needed a clearer next step, visible progress and a reason to return consistently.

03 · My role

From product definition to launch and iteration.

I owned the product roadmap, PRDs, backlog and cross-functional delivery. I worked with design and engineering to shape the learning journey, define the MVP and turn discovery findings into testable product changes.

After launch, I used onboarding and weekly retention behaviour to prioritise the next iteration rather than treating the first release as final.

04 · Key decisions

Make progress understandable and worth returning to.

01

Simplify the path to first value

The onboarding journey was sharpened so learners could understand where to start and move into useful content with less uncertainty.

02

Combine learning with practice

Exercises helped users move from reading concepts to applying them, creating a clearer signal of practical progress.

03

Use motivation carefully

Streaks and XP made continuity visible, but they supported the learning goal rather than becoming a separate game.

04

Test value and willingness to pay

Pricing surveys and premium-feature testing helped the team understand which parts of the experience users valued enough to purchase.

05 · Measurement

Track the first success and the habit that follows.

Onboarding conversion measured whether learners could move successfully into the product. It increased from 22% to 31%, a relative improvement of 41%.

Weekly active retention measured whether users found enough continuing value to return. It increased from 34% to 49%, a relative improvement of 44%.

06 · What I learned

Engagement mechanics work when they reinforce real progress.

Streaks and XP can encourage a return visit, but they cannot replace a useful learning path. The strongest improvements came from connecting motivation, practice and a clear next step into one experience.
