Case study 01 · Healthcare AI

iTrails

A GenAI and RAG platform that helps hospitals identify patients who may be eligible for clinical trials, with evidence kept visible for human review.

Product
B2B SaaS + on-premise software
Market
US Healthcare
Role
Product Manager
Scope
Discovery to launch
75%

Primary outcome

Patient review time fell from 180 seconds to 45 seconds.

The workflow reduced repetitive manual reading while keeping the final eligibility decision with trained hospital staff.

In this story The problem My role The product Key decisions Measurement What I learned

01 · The problem

Eligibility review was slow and cognitively heavy.

Clinical trials use detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria. Hospital teams had to read a patient’s history, compare each criterion and decide whether the patient should be considered. A typical review took around 180 seconds per patient.

The opportunity was not to replace clinical judgement. It was to reduce repetitive work, make relevant evidence easier to find and help staff reach a review-ready answer faster.

The product principle was simple: AI could support the decision, but a human reviewer must remain accountable for it.

02 · My role

End-to-end ownership across product and delivery.

I owned the product area from understanding hospital and pharma needs through requirements, workflow design, backlog prioritisation, engineering delivery, UAT and impact measurement.

I worked with engineering on the RAG-based workflow, with hospital stakeholders on deployment and acceptance criteria, and with business leaders on product scope and launch priorities.

03 · The product

One workflow from patient history to reviewable evidence.

iTrails connects pharma trial requirements with hospital patient information. For each patient, the system works with one consolidated patient-history document and evaluates it against a trial’s inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Patient historyA single consolidated record enters the workflow.
Relevant retrievalRAG finds the sections related to each criterion.
Criteria evaluationThe LLM checks inclusion and exclusion conditions.
Human reviewStaff see the candidate result with supporting context.

The output was designed as a review aid: candidate status, an explanation and the relevant evidence. Missing or contradictory information was surfaced instead of being silently treated as a confident answer.

04 · Key decisions

Trust and workflow fit mattered as much as model capability.

01

Keep a human in the loop

Because generative AI is non-deterministic and the workflow affects clinical-trial screening, the product supported rather than replaced the trained reviewer.

02

Show the evidence

Eligibility status alone was not enough. Reviewers needed the relevant patient context and an explanation so they could verify the result.

03

Design explicit uncertainty states

Missing and contradictory information were treated as product states requiring review, not as edge cases to hide.

04

Support hospital deployment constraints

On-premise deployment, role-based permissions and careful handling of patient data were built into the workflow. HIPAA expectations shaped privacy controls, while HL7 informed healthcare data exchange.

05 · Measurement

Measure the task, not the model in isolation.

The baseline was the time hospital staff needed to review a patient manually: about 180 seconds. During UAT, we measured the same end-to-end task with iTrails, including reading the generated evidence and confirming the result.

The completed review took about 45 seconds, a 75% reduction. This made the outcome easy to connect to the real workflow rather than relying only on technical model metrics.

06 · What I learned

In high-stakes products, explainability is part of the experience.

A technically correct answer is not useful if the person responsible cannot understand or verify it. The product became stronger when evidence, uncertainty and human review were treated as core workflow requirements rather than compliance checks added later.
